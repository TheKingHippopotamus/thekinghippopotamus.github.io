Case study

Data acquisition at scale

Eighteen upstream sources become 33 collector modules and 54 databases; a gate makes an ingestion hole an illegal claim on any surface it names.

Context

A research campaign run under my own method wrote the sentence the company did not disclose this over holes in my own ingestion. Eight reports — seven plus a baseline — came down, and the classification of that failure is the argument for everything on this page: of 19 findings, 3 were genuine non-disclosure. Five were present at the source and absent from the platform. Eleven were sitting in the platform and had never been retrieved.

So the failure the estate is built around is not a dead feed. It is absence of data mistaken for knowledge about the world. Eighteen upstream sources, 33 collector modules and 54 stores are the easy part; the part that took the design work is a nightly contract that measures what the estate actually holds, names its holes in machine-readable form, and a gate that turns a named hole into a refusal at the call site.

What I built

A source is admitted on paper before it is coded. The admission card answers five things: which data type the module owns, which endpoints were exercised live rather than read from documentation, what storage shape the rows land in, what cadence the module runs at, and — the column that does the most work — what I am not taking from this source but could. A chosen absence and an unnoticed absence are different objects, and only the first one can be governed.

Modules are one per data type, not one per vendor. SEC EDGAR alone decomposes into twelve: spine, XBRL facts, dimensioned facts and notes, insider transactions, institutional holdings, stakes, proxy compensation, material events, subsidiary trees, fails-to-deliver, full text, request logs. Each has the same shape — constants in one file, parsers that fail loud, collect / verify / status verbs with exit codes 0, 1, 2, 3, and a suite that runs offline against recorded upstream responses.

Identity is where the design paid for itself. Company identity originally hung on one primary-ticker column, so a registrant with two share classes silently lost one, and calls filed under the second class anchored to nothing. An append-only identifier registry — 10,429 rows over 3,558 entities, 17 of them carrying more than one ticker — moved coverage of speaking companies from 3,479 of 3,495 to 3,495 of 3,495. The residual 16 were never missing companies. They were corporate actions, and they now resolve with date windows.

The coverage gate. There is no arrow around it.

Decisions

  • Chose one collector module per data type over one module per vendor, because a schema change or a block then lands on one module and one store instead of on everything that source supplies — SEC EDGAR alone decomposes into twelve modules, each with its own resume state and its own circuit breaker.

  • Chose calendar-anchored discovery over provider-listing discovery, because a what-is-new surface reports what the provider is showing, not what happened — so a collector reconciles against an independent expectation source (an earnings calendar, the filing spine, a trading-day calendar) and chases the difference rather than trusting the index.

  • Chose a refusal at write time with no override parameter over a warning in the report template, because a warning is dismissed by the same person under the same deadline that produced the withdrawal — and a contract older than 36 hours is treated as no contract at all, so a stale measurement cannot quietly authorise the claim.

Limitations

  • Not a real-time system. Price grain is daily, the options surface is three snapshot windows per session, and there is no tick data and no streaming feed anywhere in the estate.

  • Not full history everywhere, and the gaps are stated rather than smoothed: dimensioned XBRL for fiscal 2015-2019 is not loaded and waits on storage, 8-K narrative bodies are 108,006 of 342,275, stake coverage has a 2023-01-01 floor by decision and fails-to-deliver a 2009-07 floor. The 1984 price floor is deep but sparse — it is not dense daily coverage of a wide universe, and any backtest must state its own measured start date rather than inherit the table's minimum.

  • No uptime, no SLA and no data-quality percentage. None was measured; the estate publishes named holes instead of a score. The 7,048 test functions were counted, not run, so no pass rate is claimed either. This is a private research estate collecting from public and permitted surfaces — not a licensed or redistributable market-data product.

  • Live transcript coverage begins 2026-07-23. Everything earlier exists only in a frozen archive that is no longer collected, and the corpus is not a complete record of the market's earnings calls.

  • The module contract requires a schema contract, crash-resume, a circuit breaker and an offline suite from every module — but 19 of the 54 stores currently carry an explicit resume-state or run-log table, so that is the contract, not a measured property of all 33 modules.
