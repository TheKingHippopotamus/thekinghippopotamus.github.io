Case study

Research & discovery method

Seven stations from vision to falsification. The seventh turned on my own work: eight reports destroyed, none republished, because 99% reliable is not kept.

Context

I do not start research by asking a question and hunting for an answer. I write the programme down first, and the shape has come out the same way six times across six unrelated programmes — a filing-forensics system, an earnings-call platform, a deterministic equity workbench, two corpus-discovery studies and a platform blueprint — written months apart, in different domains.

The reason is a specific failure mode. A research system that cannot tell “the company never disclosed it” from “we never went and got it” does not produce incomplete work. It produces confident work that is wrong, because every ingestion hole is rendered in the deliverable as a disclosure hole and the sentence reads the same either way.

So the programme is written as artifacts that can refuse: an objective matrix a deliverable is checked against rather than a prose brief, a source census whose most useful column is what I am not taking, an execution manifest that gives every unit a budget and one output path, and gates that exit non-zero. The last station is the one that costs something — treating a finished report as a hypothesis and running the audit against myself.

What I built

The method is seven stations, and the seventh is the one that matters.

In August 2026 I put a finished campaign — 8 reports (7 plus a baseline), 7 tickers, 66 declared limitations — through a falsification pass that asked one question of every declared gap: genuinely not disclosed, present at source but never ingested, or sitting in the platform and never retrieved? The tally came back 19 findings — 3 non-disclosure, 5 missing from the platform, 11 present and unread. The third class is the dangerous one: there a stated limitation is a misdescription, because the report says *cannot be known* when it means *we did not look*. I destroyed all 8, rebuilt the site to zero posts and closed the domain. A report that is 99% reliable is not kept.

That question had already exposed a defect one layer down. One INNER JOIN against the question-and-answer table made 55.8% of the corpus text — 274,961,510 characters — unreadable to every stage of the programme: precisely the half where a company states its own capacity, customers and constraints before any analyst speaks. 47% of a later study's cited material came from that half.

The first six stations are what make the seventh survivable — a dated vision document, a frozen objective matrix (66 rows across 33 themes), a source census with an explicit gap column, an execution manifest of 48 units with budgets and fixed output paths, five gate programmes exiting 0 pass / 1 block / 2 warn-and-acknowledge, and a deliverable that ships its own limits.

The programme lifecycle — seven stations, gates between them, and the return arrow

Decisions

  • Chose gates that return an exit status over a prose quality checklist, because a checklist cannot refuse to pass work — five gate programmes sit at the seams of a run and exit 0 pass, 1 block, 2 warn-and-acknowledge, and the analysis units are required to read the second gate's result file and answer its warnings in their own reasoning.

  • Chose a consolidated facts layer with disk-only analysis units over letting every unit read the raw filing extract, because 780 KB of source facts per company does not fan out to thirteen readers — consolidation reduces it to a 30–50 KB indexed document and the analysis units are forbidden the extraction tooling entirely, which also frees them from the single processing lock that serialises the run.

  • Chose to destroy 8 finished reports over correcting and republishing them, because the audit could not separate genuine non-disclosure from never-retrieved for every one of their 66 declared gaps, and a report that is 99% reliable is not kept — the failure record was preserved on purpose so the replacement process is built on what went wrong rather than on memory of it.

Limitations

  • This counts specifications, code and stored artifacts. It does not audit execution history — nothing here establishes how many companies completed a full gated run, or any success rate. The method is shown as design and as one falsification pass, not as a throughput record.

  • The pipeline runs at Level 1, manual trigger. There is no autonomy claim, and the units inside it are tooling under a protocol I wrote, not an unattended system.

  • Three of the six programmes are unfinished: the earnings-call platform is specified and partially built; the corpus-emergence study has a method built and run but no written note or rendered page; the workbench's own README still lists compute frameworks blocked on remaining adapters and a render phase pending.

  • The supply-chain graph is not validated. Its audit verdict is structurally sound and economically about 90% valid, not fit to ship as-is, with named backwards edges, one exact duplicate and 5 missing edges that break exactly the read-through the platform wants.

  • The corpus studies measure language, not orders — a company can talk shortage to justify price and can stay silent about a real one. Nothing here is a market, causal or investment claim. The withdrawn reports were destroyed, not corrected: the site was rebuilt to zero posts and nothing was restored.
